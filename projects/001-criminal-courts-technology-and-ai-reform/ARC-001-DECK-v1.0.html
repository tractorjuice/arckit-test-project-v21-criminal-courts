Executive Summary · The Answer

Proceed with the £281M Phased Comprehensive Reform — payback in 42 months and a 1.5:1 benefit-cost ratio outweigh the cost of inaction.

Backlog reduction alone delivers £180M of the £420M ten-year benefit. Phased delivery with stage-gate Treasury approvals manages risk while enabling early benefit realisation.
Source: ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0 (Strategic Outline Business Case) 02 / 14
Situation

The criminal courts are in crisis on four fronts simultaneously — backlog, delay, legacy, and fragmentation.

Drawn from the Independent Review of the Criminal Courts (2025-2026) and stakeholder analysis across 22 organisations.
Source: Leveson Review Part 1 (Jul 2025) & Part 2 (Feb 2026); ARC-001-STKE-v1.0 03 / 14
Complication · Why Now

Without intervention, the crisis compounds — and the political window for action is closing.

Five forces accelerate the cost of inaction; cross-party Leveson support is the only realistic opening to act.
Source: NAO Report on Government Legacy IT (2024); ARC-001-RISK-v1.0 04 / 14

Six investment themes deliver the Leveson Review mandate within the £281M envelope.

Roadmap

Four phases over five years, each gated by an explicit Treasury or governance approval.

Stage gates de-risk delivery; each phase must demonstrate value before the next is funded. AI pilots run prosecution AND defence simultaneously from Phase 1.
Source: ARC-001-STRAT-v1.0 §Phase Summary 06 / 14

Investment & Return

£281M invested over five years returns £420M of benefits over ten — with backlog reduction the largest single line.

NPV £95M at 3.5% discount; BCR 1.5:1; payback 42 months. Benefits exclude unquantified gains in public confidence and judicial system legitimacy.
Source: ARC-001-SOBC-v1.0 §Economic Case (Green Book 5-case model) 08 / 14

The Decision · Recommendation

Recommendation: PROCEED — five gating actions in the next nine months unlock Phase 1.

Items 1, 3 and 4 must complete in parallel. Resolving defence technology funding (item 3) is the binding constraint for any AI go-live.
PROCEED
The cost of inaction — escalating backlog, legacy failure risk, and constitutional challenge under Article 6 ECHR — exceeds the cost of intervention. Cross-party Leveson support and a ready Green Book business case create a narrow window. Phased delivery with stage-gate approvals contains risk while delivering early value.
